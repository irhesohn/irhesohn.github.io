## Korean Cinema: Cinema and the Masses

A survey of Korean film history through cinema's relationship with its mass audiences. From the colonial period to the present, the course follows how film developed as popular entertainment and as an art form, and asks what is "Korean" about Korean cinema. Weekly screenings run from silent-era films to contemporary blockbusters.

## Women and Narration in Modern Korea

A colloquium on modern Korean history from women's perspectives. Moving from the 1920s to the present, each unit pairs a historical moment with a female figure, from the colonial-era New Woman to the women of the democratization era and of multicultural Korea. Readings and films ask who gets to narrate, and how.

## Democracies in Korean Film and Media

A colloquium on how film and media have reflected, challenged, and reshaped ideas of democracy in South Korea. Topics include political resistance, civic participation, freedom of expression, and social justice.

## Audiovisual Translation of Korean Media

An advanced Korean course on translating film and television between Korean and English. Students subtitle short scenes, complete a dubbing project, and study what changes when speech becomes text on screen.

## Special Studies

**Translating Korean Poetry.** A guided translation project built on close work with contemporary Korean poems, including the poetry of Kim Hyesoon.

**Japanese Documentary Film.** An independent study in the history and theory of Japanese documentary.

**Korean Science Fiction.** An independent study of science fiction in Korean literature and film, read as a critical discourse on science, politics, and the future.
